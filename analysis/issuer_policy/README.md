# Reference issuer policy for the oracle

This is a reference policy for the issuer behind `OracleScoreProvider`. It turns on-chain history
plus a costly identity into credit lines, and it is built to stay inside the protocol's
constraints by construction: Theorem 3 (history alone earns only dues), the identity-cost bound
of `docs/CREDIT_MODEL.md` section 4.2, the issuance budget charged on held lines (Theorem 2'), and
the rule that scores never read the backing graph (CI-19). The issuer is the accountable party of
delegated monitoring (Diamond 1984): it converts information into lines and answers for them. A
Chainlink CRE workflow can implement the policy later. Every claim below is either proved here, enforced by a test, or reproduced by `run.py`.

```bash
cd analysis/issuer_policy
python3 -m unittest -v test_policy   # 42 tests, about 1.5 s (one skips without eth_abi)
python3 run.py                       # about 35 s: results/*.csv, results/summary.md, figures/*.png
```

Requirements: Python 3.11 with numpy, scipy and matplotlib. `eth_abi` is optional; without it the
report is written as JSON arrays and the hex encoding is skipped. Every draw is seeded from
`BASE_SEED = 20261003` and a fixed tag (PCG64 via `SeedSequence`). Two consecutive runs give
byte-identical CSVs.

## 1. The policy

The issuer runs one cycle a month. Each step is a pure function of what it reads.

**Read.** For each account: `isKYCVerified`, `defaultedLoans`, and its own loans (`getBorrowerLoanIds`,
`getLoan`, `getLoanTerms`: principal, term, disbursedAt, repaid, status). Two values per loan come
from events: the closing time (the block of the last `RepaymentApplied`, or `LoanDefaulted`), and
the most secured backing the account itself held while the loan was open (the `Backed` events that
name it as borrower, summed over backers, keeping only the total). From the issuer's own
registry: the account's identity and tier. From the provider: scores, `budgetHeld`, `totalHeld`,
`epoch`, `maxTotalScore` and `maxIncreasePerReport`.

**Step 1, eligibility.** A line needs `isKYCVerified`, a costly identity in the registry with a
tier t, the identity's live address (one address per identity), and no default on any address of
the identity. Anything else gets line 0.

**Step 2, evidence.** Each closed loan of the account itself is a fractional Bernoulli trial.

$$w_{\text{good}} = \underbrace{2^{-\text{age}/h}}_{\text{recency}}\cdot\underbrace{\min\Big(1,\ \frac{\text{principal}-\text{secured}}{P_{\text{ref}}}\Big)}_{\text{unsecured size}}\cdot\underbrace{\min\Big(1,\ \frac{\min(\text{elapsed},\ \text{term})}{30\ \text{days}}\Big)}_{\text{time at risk}}$$

and $w_{\text{good}}=0$ if the loan closed inside the 24-hour interest-free window or paid no
interest. A loan repaid after its due date is a bad trial with weight
$2^{-\text{age}/h}\min(1,\text{principal}/P_{\text{ref}})\min(1,\text{term}/30\ \text{days})$.
Stake does not discount a bad trial, so stake cannot hide lateness. Open loans are not evidence
yet. A default ends the identity, so it never enters a posterior. Loans of other accounts,
including accounts this one backed, are never read.

**Step 3, PD.** A cycle is bad when the loan is late or defaults. Its rate $\theta$ has a Beta
prior per tier, fitted by moments to the tier's PD population. Since every observed account is
alive, its likelihood is $(1-\theta)^G\,\theta^B$ up to a constant, so the posterior is

$$\theta \mid \text{history} \sim \mathrm{Beta}(\alpha_t + B,\ \beta_t + G),\qquad B=\sum w_{\text{bad}},\ G=\sum w_{\text{good}}.$$

A bad event is a default with probability $1/(1+c)$, where c is the late ratio (late repayments
per default). The default probability per 30-day cycle is $p = E[\theta]/(1+c)$.

**Step 4, line.** In normal times a borrower draws $\min(\ell, D)$ with D exponential of mean
$\mu$. Before a default it draws the whole line, and LGD is 100%. With i the risk premium per
cycle (`riskPremium` × 30/365), expected issuer profit per cycle is

$$\Pi(\ell) = i\,\mu\,\big(1-e^{-\ell/\mu}\big) - p\,\ell,\qquad \ell^* = \mu\ln(i/p)\ \text{ if } p<i,\ \text{else } 0.$$

The marginal profit of the x-th USDC of line is $i e^{-x/\mu} - p$: the premium it earns only
when used, against the loss it adds at a default, when it is always used. The target is
$\min(\ell^*, \text{cap}_t)$. An account with an overdue loan gets 0 at once.

**Step 5, budget and report.** Lowering a line is free and immediate. Raises are cut into 1 USDC
segments, split at the account's held budget H. A segment between the current score S and H
costs only the per-report raise cap. A segment above H also costs new budget. The greedy takes
segments in order of marginal profit and skips what no longer fits. Changes go out in batches of
at most 500 users with consecutive epochs. Before publishing, the plan is applied to two copies
of the provider: the state as read, and the state with every held budget released to its score.
A revert in either is a bug and stops the cycle. Inside `maxScoreAge` the issuer sends empty
reports as heartbeats; they never touch the budget.

## 2. Properties

Each property has a test class in `test_policy.py`.

### P1. Theorem 3 compliance (`TheoremThreeCompliance`)

**Claim.** An account without a costly identity gets line 0 whatever its history. History alone
earns only dues, which the contract adds itself.

**Proof.** Step 1 returns 0 before any history is read, so the history rule is $f(h)=0\le\pi(h)$
for every h. Theorem 3's farming profit $n(f(h)-\pi(h))$ is then at most 0. This is the on-chain form of
"pay your dues" for cheap pseudonyms (Friedman and Resnick 2001). With a costly
identity, a history that costs nothing (grace-window loans, stake-secured loans, a guarantor
record) has weight 0 by step 2, so it earns exactly what the identity alone earns.

**Checked.** A perfect 48-loan unsecured history without the KYC flag gets 0. The flag without a
registry tier, or a tier without the flag, gets 0. Theorem 3's construction (one 100 USDC stake
recycled through 16 fresh accounts, four loans each, inside the window or over 30 days) gets 0
for every account, and its 30-day variant earns only its on-chain dues. In the simulation an
unverified farm account repays 96 loans and 9,600 USDC inside the window. The reviewer's "25% of
repaid principal" rule would give it 100 USDC; this policy gives 0. The same farm run on a bought
basic identity gets 6.10 USDC, exactly the line of a fresh basic identity with no history.

### P2. Identity-cost bound (`IdentityCostBound`)

**Lemma (lifetime loss per identity).** For any account a and any sequence of operations,
lenders' total loss attributable to a, meaning the credit charged to a as a backer plus the
residual of its own defaults, is at most $\hat\ell(a) + d(a)$. Here $\hat\ell(a)$ is the highest
line a has ever held and d(a) its dues.

*Proof.* Take Theorem 2's per-account invariant with cumulative charges:
$J(a): r(a)+c(a)+\lambda(a)+R(a)\le\hat\ell(a)+d(a)$, where R(a) is the residual lenders absorbed
at a's own defaults. It holds at the start, when every term on the left is 0. Granted credit is
$G=\max(0,\ell+d-\lambda)$, and $\lambda$ never resets. A borrow leaves either $r=0$ or
$r+c\le G$, so $r+c+\lambda\le\ell+d$ whenever r grows. A new commitment x obeys $x\le G-c$ and
$x\le\mathrm{Lim}-o$, which gives the same bound. A charge lowers c by at least what it adds to
$\lambda$. A default moves the residual from r to R (Theorem 2's case check). A lower line leaves
$\hat\ell$ unchanged. Repayment and dues only help. So J holds throughout, and the loss
$\lambda+R$ is at most $\hat\ell+d$. $\square$

**IC condition.** A profit-seeking attacker buys $n_t$ identities in tier t at $k_t$ each and
then follows any strategy. Its net profit is at most

$$\sum_t n_t\big(\min(\text{cap}_t,\ \texttt{maxLoanAmount}) - k_t\big)\ +\ \big(d - I + \text{lender recapture}\big)\ +\ \text{honest backing received}.$$

The first term is the lemma. In the second, I is the interest it paid, $d=rI$ its dues, and a
lender share s recaptures at most $s(1-f-r)I$, so the term is $I(r-1+s(1-f-r))\le -fI\le 0$
(`CREDIT_MODEL.md` 4.1). The third is honest backers' own risk, charged to their credit
(`CREDIT_MODEL.md`, "What is left to attack"). So the condition is:

$$\min(\text{cap}_t,\ \texttt{maxLoanAmount}) \le k_t\quad\text{for every tier } t.$$

Under it, a profit-seeking attacker gains nothing from any number of bought identities, through
any history. The policy enforces $\ell\le\text{cap}_t$ at every report, and
`incentive_compatible` checks the condition.

**Byzantine bound.** An attacker that does not care about profit can still cost lenders up to
$\sum_t n_t\,\text{cap}_t$ on issued lines, plus dues it paid itself into the reserve. With money
M it can buy at most $M/k_t$ identities in tier t, so under the IC condition the loss is at most
M. At any moment, the potential loss on all oracle lines is also at most
`maxTotalScore` × `maxLoanAmount` / `SCALE` (Theorem 2'). New budget enters at most
`maxIncreasePerReport` per report.

**Checked.** All default tiers satisfy the condition. Random histories never exceed a cap. For
each tier, every bust-out month (0 to 23) of a perfect borrower stays below $k_t$. An identity
that commits its whole line to a fresh account which defaults, and then defaults itself, costs
lenders at most its highest line plus its dues. In the simulation, the loss on issued lines stays under
$n\cdot\text{cap}_t$.

### P3. Bayesian PD from risk lenders bore (`Evidence`)

**Claim.** The posterior is Beta-Binomial with a tier prior. Evidence counts only risk lenders
actually bore, and every zero-cost route to evidence earns nothing.

Why each weight closes a farming route (`results/evidence_routes.csv`):

| Weight | Route it closes |
| --- | --- |
| 0 inside the 24-hour window | Grace-window loans pay no interest, and the capital comes back within a day. One seed would buy unlimited trials at gas cost (Theorem 3; `sybil_sim` attack A4). |
| unsecured share only | Stake is slashed back to lenders at a default, so a stake-secured loan puts nothing at risk, and the stake can be recycled through account after account. Fully secured weighs 0; 95 of 100 secured weighs 0.5. |
| own loans only | A guarantor record is farmable: back your own fresh account with 1 USDC of stake and let it borrow and repay. Loans of accounts it backed are never read. |
| size up to `ref_principal` | There is no minimum loan, so many dust loans would give many trials for almost no interest. |
| time at risk up to 30 days | A loan closed a day after the window would count as a full trial. Weighting by time at risk makes evidence cost interest at a fixed rate: about 1.01 USDC per trial at 100 USDC and 12.33%, and never less than 0.10 USDC at the 10 USDC reference. |
| recency, half-life 1 year | Identity markets sell aged accounts. Old evidence decays, so a bought or dormant history must be renewed with current exposure. It also limits bust-out after dormancy. |
| bad trials not discounted for stake | A borrower could otherwise hide lateness behind a secured loan. |

None of these weights is needed for the IC bound, which rests on the cap alone. The weights keep
the PD estimate honest: lines go to accounts that lenders have actually seen repay at risk, and
an attacker cannot reach a large line quickly or cheaply. Down-weighting observations by a power
of the likelihood is the standard way to discount less informative data (Ibrahim and Chen 2000).

**Checked.** Unweighted trials give the closed-form Beta posterior. The prior mean matches the
tier population. Each route above has its own test, and clean evidence lowers the PD and raises
the line monotonically.

### P4. Line sizing within every contract rule (`LineSizing`)

**Claim.** The plan maximises expected issuer profit under the tier caps, the budget, the
per-report raise cap and held budget. It never plans a report the contract would reject, even if
a `releaseBudget` front-runs it.

**Robustness lemma.** Between the issuer's read and the report landing, only `releaseBudget` can
change the provider, and it only lowers held budget, from H to the current score S. For an
account in the report with new score N, release followed by the report gives
$-(H-S)+\max(0,N-S)\le\max(0,N-H)$. So the held total after the report is at most
$\text{totalHeld} + \sum\max(0,N-H)$, as read. The contract's increase for that account rises to
at most $\max(0,N-S)$. The plan therefore enforces:

- $\sum_a \max(0,N_a-S_a)\le$ `maxIncreasePerReport`, per monthly cycle across all batches;
- $\text{totalHeld}+\sum_a\max(0,N_a-H_a)\le$ `maxTotalScore`;
- no raise at all when governance has cut `maxTotalScore` below `totalHeld`, since a release
  would turn even a raise within held budget into an increase checked against the whole total;
- $0\le N_a\le\min(\text{cap}_t,$ `SCALE`$)$, integer, at most 500 users per report, epochs
  increasing.

A lowered line keeps its budget held until the pool shows it unused, exactly as Theorem 2'
requires. Raising it back up to H costs no new budget.

**Optimality.** Every segment uses the raise cap, and only segments above H use the budget. The
greedy stops taking budget segments at some marginal profit $m_b$, and all segments at
$m_i\le m_b$. Those are the KKT conditions of the linear programme over segments, with
multipliers $\lambda_{\text{inc}}=m_i$ and $\lambda_{\text{bud}}=m_b-m_i$. So the greedy is the
optimum up to integer rounding. On 200 random instances with both caps binding, its gap to scipy's
HiGHS solution is below 0.0001%.

**Checked.** The closed-form line matches a grid search, and a lower PD never gets a smaller line.
200 random provider states respect every rule, including after a full front-run release. A naive
plan that treats a raise back to held budget as free reverts after a front-run, while the policy's
plan lands. Held budget is used before new budget. 1,234 accounts split into three batches with
consecutive epochs. A defaulting account's budget stays held until `markDefaulted`, then frees.

### P5. No backing graph (`NoBackingGraph`)

**Claim.** Scores never read the backing graph (Cheng and Friedman 2005; `CREDIT_MODEL.md`
section 1). The policy never sees who backs whom, unsecured backing, backers' scores or histories,
or any other account's loans. Its one backing-derived input is the account's own incoming secured
total per loan, and the line is non-increasing in it.

**Why that is enough.** An account's target is a function of its own identity record and its own
loans. Adding Sybil accounts or edges among them therefore cannot raise anyone's line. Sybils
without an identity get 0 and take no budget. The only way a stranger can move an honest line is
to commit stake to that borrower's open loan. That locks the stranger's stake, puts it at risk,
and can only lower the line. The budget couples accounts, since lines compete for it by marginal
profit. Only accounts with a costly identity compete, and each through its own record.

**Checked.** A staked Sybil ring, Sybils backing honest accounts, and honest accounts backing each
other leave every report and target unchanged. More secured backing never raises a line. The
policy's input type has no backing field, and the policy and allocator source never touches the
pool's backing state.

### P6. Report format (`ReportFormat`)

Each report is `(epoch, users, scores)` for `abi.encode(uint64 epoch, address[] users,
uint256[] scores)`. Epochs are consecutive integers above the provider's, users are 20-byte
addresses with no duplicates, scores are integers in `[0, SCALE]`, and lengths match. `heartbeat`
gives `(epoch + 1, [], [])`. `encode_report` returns the hex encoding when `eth_abi` is installed.
It was not installed here, so `abi_hex` in `results/report_example.json` is null and the
round-trip test skips.

### P7. Simulation (`ShortSimulation`, `run.py`)

**World.** 24 monthly reports, maxLoanAmount 100 USDC, APR 12.33% (EFFR 433 + premium 800 bps,
`DeployProduction` default), reserve share 65% (the `DeployProduction` default when this experiment was run; the default is now 45%). Honest verified borrowers: 40% basic, 40%
standard, 20% enhanced. Their true annual PDs are 1, 2, 5, 10 or 20%, drawn from the tier's
population, the same population the issuer's prior is fitted to. Each cycle they borrow with
probability 0.85. They default with the cycle PD after drawing their whole limit, repay late with
twice that probability, and otherwise repay an exponential draw (mean 50 USDC) on time. A quarter
of on-time repayments land between the issuer's read and the report. A griefer calls
`releaseBudget` on everyone just before every report lands. Identity buyers repay
`ref_principal` on time each month, then draw everything at their best bust-out month. A seed
farm runs Theorem 3's construction on unverified accounts and on one bought identity. Each
borrower's monthly draws are fixed per seed, so runs that differ only in budget or attackers face
the same borrowers (common random numbers).

**Contract rules.** 925 reports in the base and budget runs (and every report of the attack
sweep): 0 rejected by the provider port. The front-runs released 54 lines in total. The raise cap
and the budget each reached 100% use in some cycle. Unsecured exposure on oracle lines peaked at
83% of held budget plus dues, so Theorem 2' held throughout.

**Honest line growth** ([figure](figures/honest_lines.png); base run, 1,000 honest, budget 250
lines, 60 per report). Mean line of survivors, USDC:

| True annual PD | Month 0 | Month 6 | Month 12 | Month 23 | Alive at month 23 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1% | 7.4 | 24.3 | 26.1 | 28.5 | 98.7% |
| 2% | 7.2 | 24.0 | 25.6 | 27.3 | 96.5% |
| 5% | 4.9 | 19.2 | 20.0 | 21.8 | 93.8% |
| 10% | 4.0 | 17.1 | 17.9 | 17.5 | 87.6% |
| 20% | 3.0 | 14.0 | 11.7 | 9.9 | 72.0% |

Lines that start small and grow with repayment are the progressive lending of microfinance
(Morduch 1999). Month 0 is small because the raise cap rations the first report: it funds enhanced lines first,
then standard, and basic lines arrive from month 1. After the ramp, lines rise for low-PD
borrowers and fall for high-PD ones as lateness shows. The mean posterior annual PD of survivors
moves from 5.1% to 4.6% for true 1% and from 6.0% to 7.2% for true 20%.

**Calibration** ([figure](figures/calibration.png)). Over 53,935 honest borrower-cycles in three
seeds, the mean predicted default probability is 0.43% per cycle, the mean true PD 0.41%, and the
realised rate 0.40% (217 defaults, 95% CI 0.35 to 0.46%). In each predicted decile, the mean true
PD of the borrowers in it tracks the prediction within 0.05 percentage points, and the prediction
lies inside the realised rate's 95% interval in all ten deciles.

**Issuer loss rate against budget** ([figure](figures/budget.png); 600 honest, mean of 3 seeds):

| Budget (USDC) | Mean held | Loss / exposure-year | Loss / budget-year | Line-weighted true PD | Profit | Model profit |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2,500 | 2,416 | 4.23% | 3.21% | 3.40% | 128 | 135 |
| 10,000 | 9,369 | 5.49% | 3.51% | 3.83% | 266 | 308 |

A tight budget goes to the best marginal profit, so its loss rate is lowest. As the budget grows,
riskier marginal lines are funded until demand, not the budget, binds near 12,500 USDC. Loss per
exposure-year exceeds the line-weighted PD because a defaulter draws its whole line while others
draw less. Realised profit stays within about 20% of the model's expected profit.

**Attacker profit against identities bought** ([figure](figures/attacker_profit.png); 600 honest,
budget 150 lines). The attackers' best strategy is to build history until month 22 or 23 and then
draw everything:

| Tier | Line at bust | Profit per identity, true cost k_t | Profit per identity, true cost k_t / 4 | Loss on issued lines, 64 identities | Bound 64 · cap_t |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| basic (k 15, cap 15) | 14.6 | −1.0 | +10.2 | 934 | 960 |
| standard (k 40, cap 40) | 31.0 | −9.8 | +20.2 | 1,970 | 2,560 |

When the caps respect the IC condition, profit is negative and falls linearly with the number of
identities: −67, −641 and −4,769 USDC at 64 identities. When the issuer has overestimated
identity cost fourfold, so that caps are four times the true cost, profit is positive and linear:
+653, +1,279 and +991 USDC at 64 identities. The policy is the same in both. Only the price of an
identity changed, which is why $k_t$ must be set from the market price of identities and revisited.

**Base run economics** (mean of 3 seeds): honest losses 1,447 USDC, losses on 24 bought identities
792 USDC, premium income 2,063 USDC. Realised profit on the honest book is 616 USDC against the
model's expected 568.

## 4. Limitations

- **Identity cost is an input, not an estimate.** Everything in P2 is conditional on $k_t$. If
  identities get cheaper, or one identity can be used at many lenders, the effective cost per
  identity here falls and caps must fall with it. The attacker figure shows what happens when they
  do not.
- **One identity, one person.** Deduplication across addresses is the identity provider's job. The
  policy enforces one live address per identity and ends the identity on any default. Key
  rotation would need an explicit move that carries the old address's history.
- **Thin data.** A live account can only show on-time and late repayments, and defaults end it.
  After 24 months an active borrower has about 15 trials of evidence against a prior worth 57 to
  94, so the posterior moves a fifth of the way or less, and lines discriminate by tier more than
  by individual PD. Real issuers add off-chain data, and this policy has a place
  for it only through the tier and its prior.
- **The prior is assumed right.** The simulation draws honest PDs from the same population the
  prior is fitted to. A misspecified prior biases lines in the direction of the error until
  evidence accumulates.
- **The late ratio is assumed known**, and the simulation generates lateness with the same ratio.
- **Exponential demand and full drawdown at default** are modelling choices. They give a concave
  profit and a closed-form line, and the realised profit stays within about 20% of the model.
- **Honest backing is outside the IC bound.** An attacker can also persuade honest backers. That
  is their risk, charged to their credit.
- **The CI-21 residual case.** A dominant lender can shift part of a stress loss through the pooled
  reserve. This is the contract's open issue, and it adds at most $rI$ per identity.
- **Principal only.** As in Theorem 2, interest a defaulter never pays is lost income, priced by the
  premium.
- **Not modelled:** score staleness (the issuer must send a heartbeat within `maxScoreAge`), the
  liquidity cap and buffer, impairment, and the oracle's forwarder and workflow pinning. The pool
  model covers limits, backing, stake, repayment, dues and defaults.
- **Owner overrides are not budgeted** (CI-6). This policy covers only the oracle's lines.
